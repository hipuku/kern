The finished screen lives in **Templates → AppShell → A complete view**. Everything below is
what it is made of.

## 1. Atoms, the primitives

Atoms depend on tokens and nothing else. On their own they do one small job.

<div className="flex flex-wrap items-center gap-4 p-6 my-4 rounded-card border border-line bg-surface-raised">
  <Label>Selector</Label>
  <Input value="nav > ul li.active" readOnly className="max-w-xs" />
  <StatusChip colour="orbit">class</StatusChip>
</div>

A `Label`, an `Input`, a `StatusChip`. None of them knows anything about the others, and none
reaches up to a molecule or a view.

## 2. Molecules, atoms plus a job

A molecule is a few atoms wired together so the wiring can't be got wrong. `Field` composes a
`Label` and whatever control you hand it, generating the `id` and `aria-describedby` so the
label, the control and the error can never disagree:

<div className="p-6 my-4 rounded-card border border-line bg-surface-raised">
  <Field label="Selector" hint="Specificity is highest-wins per axis, never a carry.">
    {(control) => <Input value="nav > ul li.active:not([hidden])::before" readOnly {...control} />}
  </Field>
</div>

`Metric` composes the `Card` atom with a `Label` to make one figure in a results row; `StatCard`
adds a `StatusChip` and a line of context. Same atoms, arranged for different jobs:

<div className="grid grid-cols-2 gap-3 p-6 my-4 rounded-card border border-line bg-surface-raised">
  <Metric label="[b] classes" value={2} valueClassName="type-h4 font-mono text-orbit" />
  <StatCard label="Specificity" value="0,2,3" badge="class-heavy" badgeColour="orbit" sub="Two classes, three types." />
</div>

The point is that neither `Metric` nor `StatCard` re-implements a surface, a label or a chip.
They *name* the atoms they need. If the `Card` surface changes, both change with it, which is
the whole return on building down a layer instead of across.

## 3. Organisms, a region that owns itself

An organism is a whole region of the interface, and it may own local state. kern has one,
`AppSidebar`: it arranges the `SocialBar` and `Colophon` **molecules** and the `Logo` and
`IconLink` **atoms** into the navigation rail, and tracks which item is active.

It is deliberately the only organism. With three small tools there is exactly one region big
enough to earn the layer. Inventing more to fill the shelf would make the word mean less. See
it in **Organisms → AppSidebar**.

## 4. Templates, the arrangement

A template is page structure with the content left as slots. `AppShell` places the `AppSidebar`
organism beside a scrolling `<main>`, wraps the whole thing in a `ViewportGate` (desktop-only)
and an `ErrorBoundary`, and fills the main region with a `ViewContainer`, itself a template,
the centred column a view stacks into.

Drop the molecules of section 2 into that column and the screen is complete, without a line of
bespoke layout:

<div className="flex flex-col gap-2 my-6">
  {[
    ['Template', 'AppShell → ViewportGate + AppSidebar + main + ViewContainer'],
    ['Organism', 'AppSidebar (+ SocialBar, Colophon molecules)'],
    ['Molecules', 'ViewHeader, Field, Section, Metric, CalloutCard, DataTable, BulletList'],
    ['Atoms', 'Wordmark, Input, Label, InlineCode, BulletItem, StatusChip'],
    ['Tokens', 'colour, type, spacing, radius, motion'],
  ].map(([layer, parts]) => (
    <div key={layer} className="flex gap-4 items-baseline">
      <span className="type-annotation-sc text-ink-title w-28 shrink-0">{layer}</span>
      <span className="type-p-sm text-ink-body">{parts}</span>
    </div>
  ))}
</div>

Read top to bottom, every layer is spent on the one below it and never the reverse. That is the
only structural rule kern enforces, and the reason a change to a token, an atom or a molecule
reaches the whole system at once instead of being copied into it.
